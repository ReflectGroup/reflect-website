The builder's bill of quantities

Why tender from a measured bill.

A builder's bill of quantities is the measured scope of the project, trade by trade, prepared from the issued documents. It is the basis on which a builder tenders from known quantities rather than from allowances.

Scope certainty

The bill records what has been measured, what has been assumed and what has been carried as a provisional or coverall item. The builder enters the tender knowing where the documentation is complete and where it is not.

Traceable quantities

Every quantity in the bill can be traced to a drawing number and revision. When a figure is questioned, by the estimator, a subcontractor or the client, the source is on record.

Trade packages ready to issue

The bill is arranged by trade in the builder's own breakdown, so each package can be sent to subcontractors as issued, without re-sorting or re-measurement.

A basis for the contract

Once the tender is successful, the same quantities serve as the reference for subcontract letting, progress claims and the assessment of variations throughout the project.

Tender

Builder's Bill of Quantities

For builders

A full measurement of the architectural, structural, civil and landscape trades from the issued revision set, packaged by trade and ready for issue to subcontractors. Where the documentation is silent, the assumption adopted is stated in the bill; where an element cannot yet be measured, it is carried as a provisional item. Services trades are included as coverall items unless detailed measurement is commissioned.

Why commission it
  • The tender is priced from a measured scope rather than from allowances
  • Subcontractors receive a trade package that is complete and consistent
  • Assumptions and provisional items are visible before the bid is lodged
  • The bill becomes the reference for letting, claims and variations after award
Deliverables
  • Bill of quantities by trade, unpriced, in the builder's own trade breakdown

Estimating

For builders and developers

Rates applied to the measured quantities to produce a tender estimate the builder can submit with confidence. The estimate presents trade totals, preliminaries and margin separately, with a clear distinction between measured, provisional and coverall sums. Rates are drawn from a maintained library of recently tendered projects and adjusted for location, scale and program.

Why commission it
  • Like-for-like pricing: every trade is priced against the same measured quantities
  • A benchmark figure before subcontractor quotations close
  • Preliminaries built up to the tender program rather than applied as a percentage

Procurement

Subcontractor Quote Comparison

For builders

Subcontractor and supplier quotations levelled against the bill of quantities so that scope gaps, exclusions and duplications are identified before a trade is let. The comparison sets each quotation against the measured scope, adjusts for items omitted or priced elsewhere, and reports the recommended figure together with the reasons for it.

Why commission it
  • Quotations are compared on scope and rate, not on headline total
  • Exclusions and double-ups are found before the subcontract is signed
  • The let figure is reconciled to the estimate
Deliverables
  • Side-by-side levelling of quotations by trade
  • Schedule of scope gaps, exclusions and duplications per quotation
  • Reconciliation of quotations to the estimate
  • Recommendation with supporting commentary

Methodology · Bill of quantities

A documented basis for every quantity.

How a builder's bill is prepared. The documentation is fixed, then measured, then qualified. The sequence ensures that every quantity in the bill can be traced to a drawing number and revision, and that the client knows at all times which set of documents the bill represents.

1

Documentation received

The client forwards the architectural, structural, civil and services drawings, together with schedules, specifications and reports, and advises the tender date. A written fee proposal is issued, ordinarily within two business days.

2

Basis of proposal

Every document in the set is listed with its revision in an appendix to the fee proposal. The fee is fixed against that set. Documents issued subsequently, or a material change in scope, are the subject of a separate instruction.

3

Measurement

Each trade is measured from the plans, sections, elevations and schedules in accordance with established measurement conventions. Reinforcement is taken as detailed or at stated ratios; structural steel in bulk tonnage with a stated allowance for connections.

4

Qualification

Where the documents are silent, the assumption adopted is recorded in the bill rather than raised as a query during preparation. Temporary works, hazardous materials and undetailed joinery are carried as provisional or coverall items so that no allowance is concealed within a rate.

5

Issue and tender support

The bill is issued by trade in the client's preferred format, accompanied by the drawing register and schedule of qualifications. Queries arising during the tender period are answered by reference to the bill.

Frequently asked questions

Before the documents are sent.

What documentation is required to commence?

The architectural, structural and civil drawings; any schedules and specifications; geotechnical, survey and other consultant reports; and the date on which the tender closes. The more complete the set, the fewer provisional items the bill will contain. A fee proposal can be prepared from a partial set, with the missing documents noted.

How long does the service take?

The period depends on the scale and completeness of the project. A single dwelling may be measured and issued within a few days; a multi-storey apartment building with a complete consultant set may require several weeks. The delivery date is stated in the fee proposal for each project and is held. Because each item is measured rather than allowed for, the bill is issued faster, and sits closer to the final figure, than a competing estimate prepared by approximation.

What is the difference between a bill of quantities and an estimate?

A bill of quantities lists the measured quantity of every item by trade, without prices, so that the builder and its subcontractors may price it. An estimate applies rates to those quantities to arrive at a tender figure. Many builders commission the bill alone and price it themselves; others commission both.

Can the bill be arranged to our own trade breakdown?

Yes. The client's trade breakdown or pricing template is adopted as the structure of the bill, so that quantities transfer directly into the client's existing pricing method.

How are revised drawings dealt with?

The fee proposal records the revision of every document on which the fee is based. Drawings issued after that date are measured under a separate instruction, so that the client knows at all times which set the bill represents.

Are services trades measured?

Hydraulic services other than stormwater, electrical, mechanical, fire, lift, communications and security are included as coverall items for pricing by the relevant subcontractor, unless detailed measurement of those trades is commissioned. Stormwater drainage and tanks are measured from the hydraulic drawings.

How are fees determined?

Bills of quantities, estimates and cost plans are quoted as a fixed fee for each project, based on the documentation and scope at the date of the proposal. Reviews, revisions and advice outside that scope are quoted separately or charged at an hourly rate stated in the proposal.

Does the location of the project matter?

No. Measurement is undertaken from the issued documentation and does not depend on where the project is located.
