How we work

Five steps, in order.

The documentation is fixed first, then measured, then qualified, so that every quantity in the bill can be traced back to a drawing number and revision.

1

Send the drawings

Email the architectural, structural and services drawings with any reports and the tender date. A fee proposal usually follows within two business days.

2

Basis of proposal

Every drawing, schedule, specification and report in the set is listed with its revision in an appendix to the proposal. Later revisions or a change in scope are priced separately.

3

Measurement

Trades are measured from the plans, sections and schedules. Reinforcement is taken as detailed or at stated ratios; structural steel in bulk tonnage with a nominal allowance for connections.

4

Qualification

Where the documents are silent, the assumption is written into the bill. Temporary works, hazardous materials and undetailed joinery are carried as provisional or coverall items so nothing is hidden inside a rate.

5

Delivery and tender support

The bill is issued by trade in the format you price in, with the drawing register and qualifications attached. Questions during tender are answered against the bill.

What you receive
  • A bill packaged by trade, ready to issue to subcontractors.
  • A register of every drawing and revision the bill was measured from.
  • Assumptions, provisional items and coverall sums stated in the bill rather than raised as questions during preparation.
  • Services trades (hydraulics other than stormwater, electrical, mechanical, fire, lift, communications, security) as coverall items unless commissioned in detail. Stormwater drainage and tanks are measured from the hydraulic drawings.
  • Obvious discrepancies between drawings and reports flagged for the design team. Reflect Group does not provide a design service.
  • A typical program of 21 business days from commission, confirmed in the proposal.

Questions

Before you send the drawings.

What do you need to start?

The architectural, structural and civil drawings, any schedules and specifications, geotechnical and other reports, and the date the tender closes. The more complete the set, the fewer provisional items in the bill.

How long does a bill take?

Typically 21 business days from commission for a full builder's bill. Shorter programs can be agreed in the proposal when the tender period requires it.

What is the difference between a bill of quantities and an estimate?

A bill lists the measured quantities of every item by trade, unpriced, so you or your subcontractors can price it. An estimate applies rates to those quantities to arrive at a tender figure. Many builders commission the bill alone; others commission both.

Can you work in our template?

Yes. Send your trade breakdown or pricing template and the bill is arranged to match it, so quantities drop straight into the way you already price.

What happens when the drawings change?

The proposal records the revision of every document the fee is based on. Later revisions are measured as a separate instruction, so you always know which set the bill reflects.

How are fees set?

Bills, estimates and cost plans are fixed fees based on the documentation and scope at the time of the proposal. Reviews, revisions and advice outside that scope are charged at the hourly rates above.
