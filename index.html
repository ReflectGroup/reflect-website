The measured bill

Why tender from measured quantities.

A tender priced from a measured bill of quantities rests on a known scope. Every trade prices the same items, every quantity can be traced to a drawing, and the figure submitted on tender day can be defended when the project proceeds.

Like-for-like pricing

When subcontractors price identical measured quantities, their quotations can be compared on rate and scope alone. Differences in interpretation of the drawings are removed before they become differences in price.

Tender accuracy

Allowances and rule-of-thumb rates carry risk in both directions. A measured bill replaces them with quantities taken from the documents, narrowing the margin between the tender figure and the cost of the work.

Scope certainty

The bill records what has been measured, what has been assumed and what has been carried as a provisional or coverall item. The builder enters the tender knowing where the documentation is complete and where it is not.

A basis for the contract

Once the tender is successful, the same quantities serve as the reference for subcontract letting, progress claims and the assessment of variations throughout the project.

Methodology

A documented basis for every quantity.

The documentation is fixed, then measured, then qualified. The sequence ensures that every quantity in the bill can be traced to a drawing number and revision, and that the client knows at all times which set of documents the bill represents.

1

Documentation received

The client forwards the architectural, structural, civil and services drawings, together with schedules, specifications and reports, and advises the tender date. A written fee proposal is issued, ordinarily within two business days.

2

Basis of proposal

Every document in the set is listed with its revision in an appendix to the fee proposal. The fee is fixed against that set. Documents issued subsequently, or a material change in scope, are the subject of a separate instruction.

3

Measurement

Each trade is measured from the plans, sections, elevations and schedules in accordance with established measurement conventions. Reinforcement is taken as detailed or at stated ratios; structural steel in bulk tonnage with a stated allowance for connections.

4

Qualification

Where the documents are silent, the assumption adopted is recorded in the bill rather than raised as a query during preparation. Temporary works, hazardous materials and undetailed joinery are carried as provisional or coverall items so that no allowance is concealed within a rate.

5

Issue and tender support

The bill is issued by trade in the client's preferred format, accompanied by the drawing register and schedule of qualifications. Queries arising during the tender period are answered by reference to the bill.

Frequently asked questions

Before the documents are sent.

What documentation is required to commence?

The architectural, structural and civil drawings; any schedules and specifications; geotechnical, survey and other consultant reports; and the date on which the tender closes. The more complete the set, the fewer provisional items the bill will contain. A fee proposal can be prepared from a partial set, with the missing documents noted.

How long does the service take?

The period depends on the scale and completeness of the project. A single dwelling may be measured and issued within a few days; a multi-storey apartment building with a complete consultant set may require several weeks. The delivery date is stated in the fee proposal for each project and is held. Because each item is measured rather than allowed for, the bill is available sooner, and sits closer to the final tender figure, than an estimate prepared by approximation.

What is the difference between a bill of quantities and an estimate?

A bill of quantities lists the measured quantity of every item by trade, without prices, so that the builder and its subcontractors may price it. An estimate applies rates to those quantities to arrive at a tender figure. Many builders commission the bill alone and price it themselves; others commission both.

Can the bill be arranged to our own trade breakdown?

Yes. The client's trade breakdown or pricing template is adopted as the structure of the bill, so that quantities transfer directly into the client's existing pricing method.

How are revised drawings dealt with?

The fee proposal records the revision of every document on which the fee is based. Drawings issued after that date are measured under a separate instruction, so that the client knows at all times which set the bill represents.

Are services trades measured?

Hydraulic services other than stormwater, electrical, mechanical, fire, lift, communications and security are included as coverall items for pricing by the relevant subcontractor, unless detailed measurement of those trades is commissioned. Stormwater drainage and tanks are measured from the hydraulic drawings.

How are fees determined?

Bills of quantities, estimates and cost plans are quoted as a fixed fee for each project, based on the documentation and scope at the date of the proposal. Reviews, revisions and advice outside that scope are quoted separately or charged at an hourly rate stated in the proposal.

Where does Reflect Group operate?

Services are provided Australia wide. Measurement is undertaken from the issued documentation and does not depend on the location of the project.
